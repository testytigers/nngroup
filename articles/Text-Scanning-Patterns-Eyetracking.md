<span class="article-summary-label">

Summary:&nbsp;

</span>Eyetracking research shows that there are 4 main patterns that people use to scan textual information on webpages: F-pattern, spotted pattern, layer-cake pattern, and commitment pattern.

</div>

<div class="article-body">

<p>On the web, people  don’t read every word on a page; instead, they <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-web-users-scan-instead-reading/">scan</a>. They naturally <a href="http://www.nngroup.com/articles/lazy-users/">attempt to be efficient</a> and put in the least possible work for achieving their goal. They have learned that scanning can deliver almost the same amount of information as reading, but with significant less time and effort.</p>

</div><h2 id="toc-scanning-patterns-1">Scanning Patterns</h2>

<p>How people read on the web is highly contingent upon:</p>

<ul>

<li><a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/eyetracking-tasks-efficient-scanning/">Their task</a></li>

<li>Their assumptions from previous experiences with the internet, site, or brand</li>

<li>The page layout</li>

<li>The type of page content (e.g., text versus images)</li>

</ul>

<p>Our <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/books/eyetracking-web-usability/">eyetracking</a> research has identified multiple <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/zigzag-page-layout/">scanning patterns</a> for webpages. In this article, I focus on the <strong>4 patterns that people use to scan text </strong>on the web  (listed below in increasing order, worst to best, of effectiveness):</p>

<ul>

<li><a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/">F-pattern</a></li>

<li>Spotted pattern</li>

<li><a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/">Layer-cake pattern</a></li>

<li>Commitment pattern</li>

</ul>

<p>Note that there are other patterns that may be used when the page content involves a lot of images (e.g., the <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/zigzag-page-layout/">zigzag pattern</a> and <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/exhaustive-review-eyetracking/">exhaustive review</a>).</p>

<h2 id="toc-f-pattern-2">F-Pattern</h2>

<p>In the absence of subheadings and bullets, users tend to fixate on the words toward the beginning of lines and toward the top of the page. This scanning behavior results in an eyetracking pattern that resembles the capital letter F — hence, our name for <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/">this pattern</a>. In left-to-right languages, text on the left and towards the top of the page is read more than text on the right or towards the bottom of the page. (In <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-attention-original-research/">right-to-left languages the pattern is mirrored</a> vertically, with more attention being spent on the right side of the page.)</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="Red and yellow overlay indicating fixations in the rough shape of the capital letter F appear on a section of an article page of prose" height="602" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2019/07/29/tigersincrisis-heat_corrected_cropped_watermark.png" width="692"/>

<figcaption><em>TigersinCrisis.com: This heatmap aggregates data from 47 people as they tried to learn how many Sumatran tigers live in the wild. The red color on this heatmap is an example of the F-shaped pattern — same today as when we first reported it in 2006.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<p> </p>

</figure>

<h2 id="toc-spotted-pattern-3">Spotted Pattern</h2>

<p>The spotted scanning pattern involves fixating on specific words or chunks of words spread throughout the page. The user chooses words because one of two reasons:</p>

<ol>

<li><strong>They visually stand out in the text </strong>because they are styled differently (e.g.,  links, differently colored words, bolded words, bulleted lists).</li>

<li><strong>They resemble </strong><a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/eyetracking-tasks-efficient-scanning/"><strong>a word that the user looks for to accomplish the current task</strong></a>  (for example,  capital letters for an address, digits for a piece of numeric information).</li>

</ol>

<p>The spotted scanning pattern is slightly more operative than the F-pattern if the web designers did a <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/link-promise/">good job naming links</a>, making important words look different from the rest of the body text, and creating bulleted lists.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="A red and yellow area demarks the area with content users were looking for" height="581" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2019/07/29/wikiped_micbar_education_fpattern-spotted_6ps_ab_85rad_cropped_watermark.png" width="692"/>

<h2 id="toc-layer-cake-scanning-pattern-4">Layer-Cake Scanning Pattern</h2>

<p>The layer-cake scanning pattern consists of fixations placed mostly on the page’s headings and subheadings. There are few other fixations on the text in between — that is, until users locate the heading they are interested in; at that point, they usually read the accompanying body text below. In an eyetracking heatmap or gaze plot, the layer-cake pattern looks like a set of horizontal stripes and blank spaces between them, resembling a layer cake (with cake on a level, then frosting, then cake, and so on).</p>

<p>Aside from reading almost every word, the layer-cake pattern is by far the most effective way in which users can scan pages.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="1357" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2019/07/29/maintenence_yamaha_layer-cake_p13reuben-all_watermark.png" width="692"/>

<figcaption><em>As he was researching how to winterize a boat engine on Yamaha.com, a user scanned to the subheadings, which appeared as bold, light blue text juxtaposed against the plain, black body text.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<h2 id="toc-commitment-pattern-5">Commitment Pattern</h2>

<p>The commitment pattern demonstrates traditional reading, not scanning. In this pattern, users fixate on all or most content words in the text passage. This pattern usually occurs when users are very interested or very motivated to read the content (for example, because they are studying for a test, or need to return an item on a specific site and are reading the instructions to do so).  </p>

<p>The commitment pattern usually leads to the best <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension/">comprehension</a>, even though it is the most time consuming. People spend more time and effort reading than they do when just scanning but reading everything gives them the opportunity to glean more information. Note though, that, even for the commitment pattern, text <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/inverted-pyramid/">comprehension</a> is improved when the content is chunked and calls out its main points in subheadings. So, just because we know users may want to read or need to read certain content doesn’t give us a pass to load webpages with walls of text.</p>

<p>We said before that the commitment scanning pattern usually occurs when people are highly motivated to be on the page and learn<strong>. People are motivated when they</strong>:</p>

<ul>

<li>know and <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/commitment-levels/">trust</a> the source</li>

<li>are loyal to the <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/brand-experience-ux/">brand</a></li>

<li>believe they are in the best place to find the information (e.g., because they received aa referral, the page title matches their exact need, the description and title on the SERP match their thinking — we like to call this last phenomenon <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-skills-improving-only-slightly/"><em>Google Gullibility</em></a>)</li>

</ul>

<h2 id="toc-summary-6">Summary</h2>

<p>Eyetracking research helps us to see the details of how users look at content and how they choose to skip or read it. When you write, edit, or organize text on a webpage or in an app, keep in mind that how you present your content is likely to favor one of the four text-scanning patterns: the F-pattern, spotted, layer cake, and commitment patterns. Know that most users will read very little from a wall of text; support them by chunking your content into sections and bulleted lists, by using meaningful subheadings, and by special visual styling for keywords.</p>

<p>For more information about reading and scanning, see our full research report, “<a href="https://www.nngroup.com/reports/how-people-read-web-eyetracking-evidence/">How People Read on the Web: The Eyetracking Evidence</a>”. To do your own eyetracking research, see <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/eyetracking-setup/">how we do eyetracking studies</a> and consult our free report “<a href="https://www.nngroup.com/reports/how-to-conduct-eyetracking-studies/">How to Conduct Eyetracking Studies</a>” for more detailed advice.</p>
