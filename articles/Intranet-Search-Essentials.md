NN/g researchers conductedusability testsandfield studieson intranets at 15 different organizations around the world. A recurring theme emerged in the findings: at many organizations,intranet search wasn’t effective enough.

During an interview, one intranet team member said, “Intranet search is a huge thing for our employees and many of them prefer to search. Because of that, we have to get our search right. When people become frustrated by it, they’re not likely to engage with search or even the intranet again.” In this article, we'll outline design and process areas to focus on to improve your intranet-search experience for employees.

Employees’ initial expectations for intranet search were high. Theyexpected the intranet’s search to work like Googleand were repeatedly disappointed when it didn’t. Participants began information-seeking tasks with high hopes for the intranet’s search results, only to lose confidence when it continually failed to return productive results. Lack of success degraded trust and led employees to stop using search altogether, as they assumed it wouldn’t work. They usually turned to the navigation next to find what they needed and took much longer to complete their tasks.

Ensuring that search returns relevant results is an ongoing challenge for every intranet.Good search begins with great content that’s added when needed, removed when it’s not, and proactively updated when there are changes.Content that’s organized logically in the site’s information architecture (and menus), crosslinked from relevant pages, and accompanied by strong, careful metadata will set your search up for success.

The design of your intranet search and the processes behind it are tightly coupled. You can have all of the right design elements in place, but if the content creation,management, and publication processes don’t support the design, the intranet search will fail. Conversely, if the behind-the-scenes processes are in place, but the design excludes essential elements, the intranet design will also fall short. If left ignored, problems with search will lead employees to give up on it (and the intranet), while making incremental improvements over time will build confidence, trust, and engagement among employees.
