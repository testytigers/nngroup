In an episode of theNN/g UX Podcast, NN/g’s Director of Research Maria Rosala spoke withKate Towsey, author ofResearch That Scales: The Research Operations Handbook. Maria and Kate discussed building effective research systems and demonstrating value.

Listen to the episode.

Kate Towsey is a pioneering figure in research operations. She helped establish some of the first ResearchOps systems at the UK's Government Digital Service (GDS) and later at Atlassian. She is also widely recognized for her role in building a globalResearchOps community. With a background spanning radio journalism and content strategy, she brings a unique systems-thinking perspective to research operations.

Research operations,as Kate defines it, is about delivering “the systems that enable people to do research, […] consume research, and act on research."

It's not just about supporting researchers — it's about creating “a cohesive space” where stakeholders, participants, and research consumers can effectively share and use knowledge to make better decisions.

Kate compares research operations to civic design. Just as a city needs various buildings (libraries, banks, community centers) connected by transportation systems, ResearchOps needs various functions connected by well-designed processes.

Kate Towsey:

"The way something is shaped and how it feels […] will change how you experience and therefore how you feel and think about it — in this case, the ‘city of research.’”

According to Kate, successful ResearchOps work requires a systematic, service-design approach. When companies start working on research operations, Kate often sees them fail because they’re reacting to an immediate need (like a research library or recruitment panel) but not considering how that need fits into the larger picture of the organization’s goals. She calls this short-sighted, reactive approach “knee-jerk operations.”

Of course, every organization has to start somewhere, and it’s usually in smaller, scoped projects. "Every city started as a village," Kate said. "Hold that in mind that no one's expecting you to come and manifest Rome in one day."

Kate Towsey:

"Systems are defined as being specific [...] and you cannot create a specific system if you're not specific about what you want to achieve. That’s the number one thing.”

Having a defined strategy allows for defining clear success metrics, which is crucial for building leadership buy-in for ResearchOps work.

While AI promises new efficiencies, Kate advocated for a measured approach: "I think it's got incredible value. I still feel like it's a tool.Like all tools, you have to think about how it’s used."

She noted that once teams move past either panic or over-optimism about AI, the real work begins: carefully evaluating where AI can genuinely smooth processes, enable automation, or take over specific tasks entirely.

However, Kate highlighted a critical tension in the current landscape. While organizational appetite for AI-driven efficiency is at an all-time high, teams face practical challenges in implementation:

Kate Towsey:

"Because of […] budget cuts, [,..] the word of the moment for everybody is efficiency. It always has been, but it's now in capital letters, underscore, italics. And AI came in just at this time. It's […] unfortunate timing."

She predicted that, within six months to a year, the industry would see more "intelligent, jargon-free conversation" emerge about AI's practical applications in research operations —  identifying where it proves most valuable and where human involvement remains essential. The key, Kate suggested, was maintaining a balanced view: embracing AI's potential while thoughtfully considering where and how it fits within existing research systems and processes.

ResearchOps sits at an interesting intersection of well-established practices (knowledge management, community building, training) and cutting-edge technology. Success requires balancing both, while staying focused on the fundamental goal: enabling better decision making through research.

As Kate concluded, "The appetite for research is massive. No one's arguing that research isn't valuable."

The challenge for ResearchOps professionals is to build systems that make research more accessible, impactful, and efficient — whether that's through traditional methods or emerging technologies.
