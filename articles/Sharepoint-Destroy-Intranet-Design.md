<span class="article-summary-label">

Summary:&nbsp;

</span>As intranet projects benefit from powerful implementation platforms, teams should focus on optimizing the user experience for specific organizational needs, as 4 winning examples show.

</div>

<div class="article-body">

<p>Several participants at recent <a class="new" href="http://www.nngroup.com/training/" title="Nielsen Norman Group: usability conference and training seminars"> Usability Week conferences</a> asked me whether prospects were bleak for intranet designers given the trend for more and more <a class="old" href="/articles/10-best-intranets-of-2009/" title="Alertbox: 10 Best Intranets of 2009"> intranets to be built on SharePoint</a>.</p>

<p>It's true that it's <strong> less work </strong> to build on top of an intranet-optimized system, as opposed to developing an intranet from scratch or patching it together from a bunch of website-oriented tools. But less work doesn't mean no work. And it certainly doesn't mean no usability work.</p>

<p>The basis for all usability is to relate the design to two questions: <strong> who are the users and what are their tasks? </strong> The answers <a class="old" href="/articles/the-difference-between-intranet-and-internet-design/" title="Alertbox: The Difference Between Intranet and Internet Design"> show how intranets differ from websites</a>:</p>

<ul>

<li><strong>Users </strong> = employees instead of customers</li>

<li><strong>Tasks </strong> = work instead of shopping</li>

</ul>

<p>These differences are why it makes sense to have <a class="new" href="http://www.nngroup.com/reports/intranet-usability-guidelines/" title="Nielsen Norman Group report series: Intranet Usability Guidelines based on usability studies of 27 corporate intranets"> separate usability guidelines for intranets</a> and why products such as SharePoint exist. (Other vendors offer similar solutions; I use SharePoint here because it was the most widely used platform among winners of our <a class="new" href="http://www.nngroup.com/reports/intranet-design-annual/" title="Nielsen Norman Group report: Year's 10 Best Intranets"> Intranet Design Annual</a> usability competition. We don't endorse any specific software.)</p>

</div><h2 id="toc-intranets-differ-1">Intranets Differ</h2>

<p>Although there are high-level similarities across intranets, many differences emerge once we delve into the details. As an example, consider 4 of the <a class="old" href="/articles/10-best-intranets-of-2010/" title="Alertbox: 10 Best Intranets of 2010"> winning intranets for 2010</a> — they all use SharePoint, but they don't all look the same:</p>

<p style="text-align:center"><img alt="Homepages of 4 Intranet Design Annual winners. Top row: Huron Consulting Group and Enbridge.  Bottom row: SCANA Corp. and Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)" height="927" loading="lazy" src="//media.nngroup.com/media/editor/alertbox/4-sharepoint-intranet-homep.jpg" width="906"/></p>

<p>The differences go far beyond surface characteristics such as color scheme and choice of images. The two bottom intranets have a <a class="old" href="/articles/intranet-portals/" title="Alertbox: Enterprise Portals Are Popping"> portal feel</a>, with many widgets on the page, whereas the upper two have more of a homepage feel.</p>

<p>The intranets also deemed <strong> different tools </strong> important enough to be featured on the homepage: say, a calendar vs. a traffic map.</p>

<p>As even this small sample shows, some navigation categories — such as "About X" and human resources (HR) — are fairly <strong> common </strong> on intranets. For a bigger sample, see our analysis of <a class="old" href="/articles/intranet-information-architecture-ia/" title="Alertbox: Intranet Information Architecture (IA)">77 intranets' information architecture (IA)</a>.</p>

<p>On the other hand, there are also many notable <strong> differences</strong>. For example, only a consulting company would have a top-level menu for "Client management." And an "Interests" category is more suited to a science-based organization like JPL than many more mundane organizations.</p>

<p>The point is that even this very basic design choice is determined by the organization's nature.</p>

<p>Examining more detailed aspects of intranet design reveals further differences. For example, most intranets have an <a class="old" href="/articles/employee-directory-search-resolving-conflicting-usability-guidelines/" title="Alertbox: Employee Directory Search - Resolving Conflicting Usability Guidelines "> employee directory</a>, but the criteria used to find employees differ dramatically. <a class="old" href="http://www.nngroup.com/reports/best-knowledge-intensive-business-intranets/" title="Nielsen Norman Group report: Great Knowledge-Intensive Sector Intranet Designs - 16 Case Studies of Award-Winning Intranets from Consulting Companies, Design Agencies, Publishers, Pharmaceutical and Biotech, and Other Research-Driven Organizations"> Knowledge-intensive organizations</a> often emphasize the ability to find colleagues by their expertise, whereas other types of organizations might deem other attributes more important. Similarly, the information displayed on individual <a class="new" href="http://www.nngroup.com/reports/intranet-people-information/" title="Nielsen Norman Group report: Usability Guidelines for Corporate Information, Teams, Departments, and People"> employee profile pages</a> is not just <strong> industry-dependent</strong>, but also varies dramatically depending on <strong> company culture</strong>.</p>

<p>Clearly, there are myriad design choices left, even after a company chooses a platform such as SharePoint. And these choices can have a huge impact on employee productivity and the intranet's success.</p>

<p>In fact, the easier it gets to build the intranet's basic infrastructure, the more important it becomes to populate it with the most useful features possible and to create efficient user interfaces for those features. Intranets of the future might require less programming work, but they'll surely need even more user experience work.</p>
