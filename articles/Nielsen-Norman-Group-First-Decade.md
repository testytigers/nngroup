Summary:&nbsp;

</span>Started in 1998, the company is now 10 years old and has a long list of accomplishments.

</div>

<div class="article-body">

<p>Nielsen Norman Group was founded August 8, 1998. Whether or not you believe the Chinese superstition that "8" is the luckiest number, our company has certainly done well, and I don't think it all stems from its launch on 8/8/98.</p>

<p>I don't usually write about my own company in the Alertbox, because readers are more interested in our research <em> findings </em> than in the <em> organization </em> that enables the work. But, in celebration of our 10-year anniversary, I'll indulge in a brief glance back at our first decade's accomplishments and look forward to our next.</p>

</div><h2 id="toc-how-it-all-started-1">How It All Started</h2>

<p>Don Norman gets the credit for the vision: In the late 1990s, the world needed an elite usability company. In any other field, you could find companies at all desired levels, from cheap local consultants to world-class — albeit expensive — experts. In usability, this was not so in 1998. All the best usability experts worked at big technology companies and focused on the companies' internal needs. Don's insight was that the usability field was expanding beyond the technology industry and thus opening up the niche we were to fill.</p>

<p>As Don was mulling over the idea of launching this elite company, he read an <a class="out" href="http://www.nytimes.com/library/tech/98/07/cyber/articles/13usability.html" title="NY Times, July 13, 1998: Making Web Sites More 'Usable' Is Former Sun Engineer's Goal"> article in <cite> The New York Times</cite></a> saying that I'd left Sun Microsystems to focus on writing a <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/books/designing-web-usability/">book on web usability</a>. He called me up and suggested that we start a company together.</p>

<p>In addition to the timely newspaper article, there was a second piece of luck: We both lived in Atherton, California, a particularly snooty part of Silicon Valley that is often listed as one of the three wealthiest towns in the United States. Now, I would accept a call from Don Norman anytime, but if he hadn't lived five minutes from my house, I doubt that we would have been able to get together enough to found a start-up.</p>

<p>Despite all the Internet technology advances, sharing dinners at good restaurants and coffee at each other's houses remains the best way to build a personal connection.</p>

<p>The day we started the company, <cite> The San Jose Mercury News</cite>' technology columnist, Dan Gillmor, called Don for a quote on a story. He also wanted my comments, and asked Don if he happened to know where I could be reached. Don said, "Jakob is right here." Ever the good investigative reporter, Gillmor put two and two together and asked if we were up to something. As a result, the <cite> Mercury News </cite> ran his scoop about Nielsen Norman Group and we never got to issue a press release.</p>

<h2 id="toc-what-im-most-proud-of-3">What I'm Most Proud Of</h2>

<p>I'm certainly proud of all of the accomplishments listed above, but none of them are what I'm the <em> most </em> proud of because they're all things I knew I could do before we started the company: I have written best-selling books since <cite> Hypertext and Hypermedia </cite> in 1989, and I have taught usability courses and done usability consulting <a class="old" href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/25-years-in-usability/" title="Alertbox: 25 Years in Usability"> since 1983</a>. (One of the few benefits of being a low-paid university professor is that you're strongly motivated to get real-world industry experience on the side.)</p>

<p>I am most proud of our <strong> team </strong> at Nielsen Norman Group. It's amazing that so many incredibly talented people have become group members, and have stayed with us for so many years.</p>

<p>What will the next 10 years bring? Nobody knows. There might be some adversity, like when the dot-com bubble burst in 2001, just as Nielsen Norman Group was investing in a worldwide conference series. We made it through that just fine; in fact, we've done fine every single year.</p>

<p>Whatever the general outlook, I think the <strong> future is extremely bright </strong> for usability, for the simple reason that it <em> works </em> and has hugely profitable <a class="new" href="/reports/usability-return-on-investment-roi/" title="Nielsen Norman Group report: Return on Investment from Usability"> ROI</a> for companies that embrace it. I also think that Nielsen Norman Group is well positioned to ride this growth curve and accomplish even more in its second decade.</p>

<p>We'll of course research new issues, such as multimedia usability and <a class="new" href="/courses/usability-mobile-websites-apps/" title="Nielsen Norman Group full-day tutorial: Mobile Usability"> mobile access</a> to websites and intranets. But we'll also keep our focus on the <a class="old" href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/10-high-profit-redesign-priorities/" title="Alertbox: 10 High-Profit Redesign Priorities"> bread-and-butter issues</a> that dictate the <strong> vast majority of online profits</strong>: <a class="new" href="/courses/topic/writing-web/" title="Nielsen Norman Group 2-day training course on content usability"> writing for the Web</a>, <a class="new" href="/courses/topic/information-architecture/" title="Nielsen Norman Group 2-day tutorial on Navigation Design"> information architecture</a>, <a class="new" href="/courses/web-ux-design-guidelines/" title="Nielsen Norman Group full-day tutorial: Fundamental Guidelines for Web Usability"> making websites easier</a>, <a class="new" href="/reports/intranet-usability-guidelines/" title="Nielsen Norman Group report series: Intranet Usability Guidelines"> intranet usability</a>, <a class="new" href="https://www.nngroup.com/courses/application-ux/" title="Nielsen Norman Group 2-day tutorial: Application Usability"> application design</a>, and <a class="new" href="/topic/email/" title="Nielsen Norman Group report: Email Newsletter Usability - 165 Design Guidelines for Newsletter Subscription, Content, Account Maintenance, and RSS News Feeds Based on Usability Studies"> email newsletters</a>. Such topics will never go out of fashion — in fact, they're more important than whatever short-lived fad whips the blogosphere into a frenzy any given year.</p>

<p>The basics might be well established, but most companies still get them wrong, so we must keep pushing to <strong> make everyday user experience more suited for humans</strong>. That's what Nielsen Norman Group is all about.</p>
