<span class="article-summary-label">

Summary:&nbsp;

</span>There is room in the market for a device in-between laptops and cellphones. But Palm Foleo isn&#x27;t it. It&#x27;s too close to a laptop.

</div>

<div class="article-body">

<p>Palm has introduced a so-called "mobile companion" that can be used to work with data instead of your mobile phone. It uses the phone or WiFi for connectivity instead of having its own.</p>

<p>There is room in the market for a <strong>device in-between laptops and cellphones</strong>. But Palm Foleo isn't it. It's too close to a laptop.</p>

<p><img alt="Photo of the Palm Foleo next to a Palm Treo smartphone" loading="lazy" src="//media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2012/10/29/palm-foleo.jpg" style="width: 464px; height: 244px;"/></p>

<p><em>The Palm Foleo mobile companion with a Treo smartphone (photo from Palm's website). </em></p>

<p>It makes sense to have an information appliance with a bigger screen, bigger keyboard, and better input device instead of trying to cram email, Web, and interactive info access onto a phone. With a companion appliance for data services, mobile phones can go back to being tiny and shiny instead of clunky bricks. Of course, this would do away with Palm's own Treo phones.</p>

<p>Another aspect of the website's photo that doesn't work is showing a big mobile companion next to a big phone featuring a second keyboard. For mobile, you want to cut how much you're schlepping around, so you don't want the same feature twice.</p>

<p>The product website is miserable and doesn't provide any concrete info. Luckily the <a class="out" href="http://www.nytimes.com/2007/05/31/technology/31device.html" title="New York Times, March 31, 2007: Palm to Sell Device That Won’t Fit in the Palm, by John Markoff"> <cite> New York Times </cite> wrote about the Foleo</a> (paid access) and gave us some facts:</p>

<ul>

<li>weight: 2.5 pounds (1.1 kg)</li>

<li>thickness: 1 inch (2.4 cm)</li>

<li>size: 11x6 inches (28x15 cm) - estimated from photo</li>

</ul>

<p>Much <strong> too big and fat </strong> for a mobile device. At that size, you might as well buy a small laptop, which would run all the software you are already used to. For example Sony's Vaio TZ90 is 10% lighter and thinner than the Foleo.</p>

<p>A mobile information appliance should be thinner than 1 cm (0.4 in), weigh less than 1 pound (.45 kg), and be about 6x4 inches (15x10 cm) big. Something with these specs makes sense because it would <strong> fit the ecological niche </strong> between the laptop and the phone.</p>

<p>Palm seems ashamed of its own specs since they are nowhere to be found on the product pages.</p>

<p>This is a blatant violation of all <a class="old" href="http://www.nngroup.com/reports/ecommerce-user-experience/" title="Nielsen Norman Group: usability guidelines for e-commerce design"> guidelines for e-commerce</a>. I can't believe even the worst designer would suggest making a site where you can't find out how big the product is (especially for a mobile device). It must be a deliberate decision to hide the facts.</p>

</div><h2 id="toc-update-sept-2007-palm-cancels-foleo-project-1">Update Sept. 2007: Palm Cancels Foleo Project</h2>

<p>On September 4, 2007, <a class="out" href="http://blog.palm.com/palm/2007/09/a-message-to-pa.html" title="Palm Computer"> Palm's CEO, Ed Colligan, wrote</a>: "I have decided to cancel the Foleo mobile companion product [...] Our own evaluation and early market feedback were telling us that we still have a number of improvements to make Foleo a world-class product, and we can not afford to make those improvements."</p>

<p>Only 3 months and 5 days after I condemned the product. Palm could have saved about $10M if it had listened to usability from the start.</p>

<p>Let's hope that they will indeed invest the saved resources in making a more suitable mobile product. As I did say, there's plenty of potential.</p>

<p>An aside: the headline for Palm's announcement violates all the <a class="old" href="/topic/writing-web/" title="Jakob Nielsen: findings about content usability"> rules for writing for the Web</a> ("A Message to Palm Customers, Partners and Developers" — hard to imagine anything less specific, less SEO-friendly, and less scannable). Palm also needs some usability people on its Web team!</p>

<h2 id="toc-update-2010-tablets-can-work-2">Update 2010: Tablets Can Work</h2>

<p>The iPad (and other modern tablets) proved that I was right in 2007, when I said "There is room in the market for a device in-between laptops and cellphones." (Sadly, the <a href="http://www.nngroup.com/articles/ipad-usability-first-findings/">iPad launch apps had poor usability</a>,  but later <a href="http://www.nngroup.com/articles/tablet-usability/">tablets have been better</a>.)</p>
