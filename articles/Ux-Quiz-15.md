<li><strong>What is the difference between legibility and readability?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>No difference: they mean the same thing.</li>

<li>Legibility refers to how well people can actually see the different characters and symbols in text, whereas readability refers to how complicated the words in your text are.</li>

<li>Readability refers to how well people can actually see the different characters and symbols in text, whereas legibility refers to how complicated the words in your text are.</li>

<li>Legibility refers to how well people can actually see the different characters and symbols in text, whereas readability refers to whether people can read out loud the text on the page.</li>

</ol>

<li>Sites where users freely browse for content</li>

<li>Sites where users frequently explore different branches of the navigation hierarchy during a single session</li>

<li>Task-based sites where users rarely explore different branches of the navigation hierarchy during a single session</li>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>Opening multiple pages in rapid-fire succession as a way to save the items on those pages and revisit them at a later stage</li>

<li>Opening different sites in different tabs as the user encounters them during the day, as a way to have them available at all times</li>

<li>Bookmarking a page in the browser or in a tool like Evernote or OneNote to be able to remember it later on</li>

<li><strong>What is the difference between slips and mistakes?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>They are interchangeable terms that both mean “human error.”</li>

<li>Slips refer to human errors, and mistakes refer to software-system errors.</li>

<li>Mistakes occur when the user intends to perform one action, but instead ends up doing another (similar action). Slips occur when users have goals that are inappropriate for their tasks.</li>

<li>Slips occur when the user intends to perform one action, but instead ends up doing another (similar action). Mistakes occur when users have goals that are inappropriate for their tasks.</li>

</ol>

<li>Ask users to log in or create an account if they do not have one; do not offer a guest checkout option, but explain the benefits of registration.</li>

<li>Allow users to check out as guests.</li>

<li>Allow users to check out as guests and offer the option to register at the end of the checkout.</li>

</ol>
