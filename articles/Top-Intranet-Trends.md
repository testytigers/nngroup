<span class="article-summary-label">

Summary:&nbsp;

</span>Hero images, carousels, fat footers, video, minimalist design, and responsive navigation, are among some of the top feature trends of the best intranets of 2016. We even see a revival of online help that’s actually helpful to employees exploring new features or attempting complex tasks.

</div>

<div class="article-body">

<p>Each <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/news/item/2016-intranet-design-awards/">winning intranet</a> has its own style, feature set, and personality, unique and special in its own way. But, great minds do think alike, and some themes and features are common in multiple or all of the winning intranets in our <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/intranet-design-2016/">Intranet Design Annual 2016</a>.</p>

<p>The <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/intranet-design/">10 best-designed intranets for 2016</a> may be leading-edge cases, but the trends in their design should spread to more mainstream intranets in the next few years. Although every intranet feature won’t work well at every organization, feature trends from outstanding intranets can inspire your intranet redesign. Stay ahead of the curve and consider taking on some of the following intranet design trends now:</p>

<ol>

<li>Help and tutorials</li>

<li>Simple, minimalist-looking design</li>

<li>Better photos</li>

<li>Search evolution</li>

<li>Carousels and heroes</li>

<li>Fat footers</li>

<li>Left-side navigation</li>

<li>Social features targeted at particular topics or groups</li>

<li>Video</li>

<li>Business communication</li>

</ol>

</div><h2 id="toc-help-and-tutorials-1">Help and Tutorials</h2>

<p>After many years of being chastised for being unhelpful, online help went out of fashion. This was reinforced by the idea that an interface should stand on its own, and not need help to be usable. This year, however, we saw a resurgence of <em>Help </em>that <em>is</em> helpful. While the winning-intranets’ mostly employ nice-and-easy user interfaces, occasional more-involved or new interface elements do benefit from well-designed help. Help features can aid in discoverability of functionality, and expedite employees’ learning and understanding of the intranet and its capabilities.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="Links to tutorials on top half of page" height="1014" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/repsol_20_tutorials_live_trimmed_croppedtuts_only.png" width="720"/>

<figcaption><em>Repsol offers various support services and tutorials that explain new intranet features.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<p><span style="line-height: 1.6;">At Intermountain Healthcare, employees who need a little assistance can refer to the </span><em style="line-height: 1.6;">Help </em><span style="line-height: 1.6;">section in the right rail. Similarly, The Co-operators provides thorough, wide-ranging guidance about how to make the most of its intranet.</span></p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="409" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/navcanada_16_centralhelpwiki_live_trimmed.png" width="720"/>

<figcaption><em>NAV CANADA assists users by offering instructions about how to create a collaboration site. Such guidance and suggestions can help employees be more productive and confident as they take on new tasks.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<h2 id="toc-simple-minimalist-looking-design-2">Simple, Minimalist-Looking Design</h2>

<p>The “<a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-design/">flat</a> and boxy” designs prevalent in years past are much less pronounced this year. Most of the designs, however, continue to boast a simple, sometimes <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/characteristics-minimalism/">minimalist</a>, aesthetic.</p>

<p>Some designs, such as those from Enbridge; Repsol; and Cadwalader, Wickersham &amp; Taft, use generous white space.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/31/salini_impregilo_32_mobile-view_homepage_cropped_and-full-page_borders_transparent-back.png" width="350"/>

<figcaption><em>Left: Salini Impregilo uses a grey palette with touches of red to achieve an elegant effect with small splashes of color for excitement, as seen on the full homepage.<br/>

Right: A legible typeface and limited content makes the Salini Impregilo intranet homepage easy to scan, as seen on the first part of the homepage.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="1082" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/dorma_01_home_live_trimmed.png" width="720"/>

<figcaption><em>DORMA also uses a grey palette with touches of color.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<p>Intermountain Healthcare, NAV CANADA, Swedish Parliament, and (to a lesser degree) American Cancer Society all use rectangles for a boxy, easy-to-scan design.</p>

<h2 id="toc-better-photos-3">Better Photos</h2>

<p>Whether it indicates a strong commitment to <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/">photography</a>, more people sharing photos, or simply today’s better phone cameras, the photographs on the winning intranets are quite engaging. Photos typically relate strongly to the material they accompany and often show employees doing their work.</p>

<p>For example, the Enbridge site shows an employee evaluating a particular job site. DORMA shows two employees joking around.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="545" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/swedish-parliament_01_home_live_cropped.png" width="720"/>

<figcaption><em>Swedish Parliament features a member giving a speech. It also shows an old-but-good feature: the “Picture of the Week” in the top right corner of the homepage. In a modern twist, users can "flip" the photo over to reveal the caption and additional information about the picture.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<h2 id="toc-search-evolution-4">Search Evolution</h2>

<p><a href="https://www.nngroup.com/reports/intranet-searching/">Intranet search</a> is a lion that the best intranets tame<strong>. </strong>The <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/internal-website-search/">evolution of search</a> on this year’s winning sites is impressive.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="1437" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/enbridge_13_search_live.png" width="720"/>

<figcaption><em>Enbridge periodically asks users for feedback about the search right in the UI and collects additional feedback from content owners, site owners, and business units. The team then uses the information to improve search — such as by adjusting keywords and adding metadata. Team members also change certain results’ rankings temporarily and highlight them in “Best Bets” to elevate them in the search engine.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<p>To enhance its intranet search, Cadwalader, Wickersham &amp; Taft consolidates data sources to produce a single point of entry to knowledge resources.</p>

<p>Salini Impregilo’s search, which appears on every page, allows employees to search the entire intranet for news, <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/suggested-employee-search/">people</a>, projects, and documents.</p>

<h2 id="toc-carousels-and-heroes-5">Carousels and Heroes</h2>

<p>Although the <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-carousels/">carousel</a> is still a prominent feature on winning intranets, the hero is making a comeback. Some organizations, such as NAV CANADA and Repsol, still opt for multiple images and statements in one area, while others, such as Salini Impregilo and Intermountain Healthcare prefer one hero image to make the desired statement.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="706" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/intermountain_02_homeresponsive_live_cropped.png" width="350"/>

<figcaption><em>Intermountain Healthcare displays a hero image of a person exercising.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<h2 id="toc-fat-footers-6">Fat Footers</h2>

<p>Large footers at the bottom of pages became popular a few years ago and remain common today. Employees often know that the information they’re seeking is on the intranet, but they can’t always find it. When using a public-facing website, people may have the option to leave the site, and will. But employees often know that the intranet is the main source or the only place to turn to to find particular content.</p>

<p>Providing organized links at the bottom of pages provides employees with one more chance to locate what they need. The content in these footers can be arranged in many different ways:</p>

<ul>

<li>repeat the global navigation</li>

<li>suggest related content</li>

<li>present popular links</li>

</ul>

<p>On the NAV CANADA intranet, the wide footer navigation repeats the global navigation topics and offers a list of the megamenu links.</p>

<p>The Enbridge footer includes links to the public sites of Enbridge companies and information for contacting the Enterprise Service Desk. It also lists <em>Ethics and Conduct</em> information, along with a reminder: “<em>Let’s work together to maintain a respectful workplace</em>.”</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="1246" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/acs_06_ourworkpage_live.png" width="720"/>

<figcaption><em>The American Cancer Society's intranet footer is discernable because of the dark background juxtaposed against the light page packground.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<p>All of these visually use an obvious aesthetic element to indicate the footer; this element can be:</p>

<ul>

<li>colored background that is different from that of the rest of the page</li>

<li>border (line) delineating the footer from the page content</li>

<li>combination of the above</li>

</ul>

<h2 id="toc-side-global-navigation-7">Side Global Navigation</h2>

<p>Because mega menus don’t work in a phone UI, designers often use a <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mega-menus-work-well/">mega menu</a> for <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/killing-global-navigation-one-trend-avoid/">desktop navigation</a>, and an accordion or some other option for <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-navigation-patterns/">mobile navigation</a>. Some teams opt for menu UIs that translate easily from desktop to <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-usability-update/">mobile</a>. One such pattern popular this year is the vertical navigation bar down the left side of the page. The Swedish Parliament and Cadwalader, Wickersham &amp; Taft are among the winning organizations that follow this <a href="http://www.nngroup.com/reports/intranet-information-architecture-design-methods/">navigation</a> pattern.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="367" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/cadwalader_03_search_live_cropped.png" width="720"/>

<figcaption><em>Cadwalader, Wickersham &amp; Taft offers gloabl navigation down the left side of pages.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<h2 id="toc-targeted-social-8">Targeted Social</h2>

<p>A great trend from past years continues this year: presenting <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/intranet-social-features/">social</a> tools in an understandable, targeted way. Gone are the days of displaying a wall feed on the homepage or in personal profiles with no additional description or context. Great intranets <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/intranet-social-participation/">use social features</a> to encourage further communication about important or trending topics.</p>

<figure class="caption"><img alt="" height="572" loading="lazy" src="https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2016/05/26/salini_impregilo_28_wikiwords_technical-terms-entry_trimmed.jpg" width="720"/>

<figcaption><em>Salini Impregilo has four major social sections with clear content types; this helps users understand what is acceptable to post. These include: employee selfies, a discussion forum, a corporate-vocabulary wiki, and contests to stimulate creativity. The company’s intranet also displays the names and photos of employees who participate the most in the social feed.</em></figcaption>

</figure>

<p>DORMA’s CEO participates in the social features, leading employees by example and playing a big role in the social features’ success. Two major company events occurred near the features’ launch; these events provided a source of content, increased interest, and natural momentum for success. </p>

<p>The Cadwalader, Wickersham &amp; Taft intranet lists recent hires and employee anniversaries on the homepage. Such seemingly simple features can go a long way toward building a sense of community and inclusiveness in an organization.</p>

<p>The Co-operators offers a variety of social features, including the weekly <em>Five Minutes With</em>… (an employee-profiles feature), polls, achievements, <em>Popular Links</em>, and the ability for users to submit news. Also, in the site’s executive blogs, senior leaders share their knowledge and opinions and ask employees to do the same.</p>

<p>The <em>Trending Now</em> section on the American Cancer Society intranet summarizes the site resources, search terms, and pages that receive the most traffic. This section is a simple and automated way to keep users informed of the site’s most popular items.</p>

<p>Enbridge employees can easily access the company’s public information on social sites — including Twitter, Facebook, LinkedIn, and YouTube — via links in the middle of the homepage. This section also includes a link to the @enbridge blog. Providing quick access to external sites raises employee awareness about information that Enbridge is sharing with the public and about the conversations occurring on social-media sites.</p>

<h2 id="toc-video-9">Video</h2>

<p>Tools to create, edit, and post <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/">videos</a> have made them accessible and easy to deal with. Prevalence in social channels has lowered the expectation for <a href="https://www.nngroup.com/articles/talking-head-video-is-boring-online/">high-quality video production</a>. In fact, many people welcome the simplicity and folksiness of more realistic, just-shot-myself type of videos. With these changes, individuals, teams, and even high-level managers are taking advantage of video and sharing information in this way.</p>

<p>Videos are often stored in their own section of the intranet, which allows employees to sort, filter, and search by topic. But videos, like written content, are also presented on the homepage, in news sections, and cross linked from related contend.</p>

<h2 id="toc-business-communication-10">Business Communication</h2>

<p>Business people have learned that the intranet is the perfect place to communicate their goals and statuses to all employees. This information helps employees realize how the organization is doing and motivates them to work toward and achieve organizational goals. It also adds a level of respect, signaling to all employees that they are important enough to know where the organization has set it sights, and that that each person can play positive a role in those plans.</p>

<h2 id="toc-conclusion-11">Conclusion</h2>

<p>Whether your organization is large or small; formal or informal; public, private or government; consider which of the trends here can be implemented, deployed, and used successfully on your intranet. Choose a few to do over the next year to enhance the user experience, and increase the business value of your intranet.</p>
