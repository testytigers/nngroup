<ol>

<li><strong>What does the aesthetic-usability effect refer to?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>People tend to perceive usable products as more attractive.</li>

<li>People tend to perceive attractive products as more usable.</li>

<li>Usability and aesthetics are equally important in web design.</li>

<li>The perception of attractiveness and usability of a new site are evaluated in the first 50ms when a user first sees the site.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li><strong>Which of the following is NOT a type of microcontent?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>Page title</li>

<li>Headline</li>

<li>Tagline</li>

<li>Email body</li>

</ol>

<p id="correct2"> </p>

<br/>

</li>

<li><strong>Which of the following best describes the false-consensus effect?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>People tend to assume that others share their beliefs and responses to a given situation.</li>

<li>Designers think that their favorite web-design patterns are more widespread than they are in reality.</li>

<li>People assume that their needs are unique most of the time, and that only in exceptional situations they will react in the same way as others.</li>

<li>Members of a team tend to act cohesively to give outsiders the illusion of consensus.</li>

</ol>

<p id="correct3"> </p>

<p><button class="secondary button" onclick="correctFunction('a', 'You Are Not the User: The False-Consensus Effect', 'https://www.nngroup.com/articles/false-consensus/', 'correct3')">Reveal answer</button></p>

<br/>

</li>

<li><strong>What is a difference between a customer-journey map and an experience map?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>The customer-journey map is focused on customers, while the experience map is focused on employees.</li>

<li>The customer-journey map depicts events in chronological order, but the experience map is not chronological or sequential.</li>

<li>The experience map is agnostic of any specific products, while the customer-journey map is tied to a specific product or service.</li>

<li>The customer-journey map offers a general human perspective, not specific to a particular user.</li>

</ol>

<p id="correct4"> </p>

<p><button class="secondary button" onclick="correctFunction('c', 'UX Mapping Methods Compared: A Cheat Sheet', 'https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-mapping-cheat-sheet/', 'correct4')">Reveal answer</button></p>

<br/>

</li>

<li><strong>Which of the following best describes the exhaustive-review eye-movement pattern?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>The eyes are repeatedly drawn to the top left corner of the webpage because the user wants to make sure that the page belongs to the right site.</li>

<li>The eyes are drawn to the same area of the page repeatedly because the UI violates user’s expectations.</li>

<li>The eyes systematically visit all the areas of the page in an effort not to miss anything.</li>

<li>The eyes thoroughly scan the area of the page that has the most content.</li>

</ol>

<br/>

</li>

<li><strong>Which of the following does NOT usually appear in a service blueprint?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>Customer emotions</li>

<li>Customer actions</li>

<li>Processes</li>

<li>Backstage actions</li>

</ol>

<li><strong>Which of the following is true of quantitative research?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>It typically requires just 5 users.</li>

<li>It is used mostly for formative purposes, in the early stages of a design to inform design decisions.</li>

<li>It produces statistically meaningful results that are likely to be replicated in a different study.</li>

<li>It allows for flexible study conditions that can be adjusted from session to session according to the team’s needs.</li>

</ol>

<li><strong>Which part of a webpage receives most of the users’ fixations? </strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>Left half of the page</li>

<li>Right half of the page</li>

<li>Both sides of the page receive about an equal proportion of fixations.</li>

<li>It depends on the page level in the site’s IA hierarchy.</li>

</ol>

</li>

<li><strong>What is one advantage of sliders for application design?</strong>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>They represent a more fun and engaging input method compared with other UI controls.</li>

<li>They allow users to explore the effect of the control for the whole range of the associated parameter.</li>

<li>They support precise selection of a specific value within a range.</li>

<li>They allow both fine and coarse parameter adjustment.</li>

</ol>

<ol style="list-style-type:lower-alpha">

<li>It should be done before card sorting when both card sorting and tree testing are used.</li>

<li>It does not typically collect any quantitative measures.</li>

<li>It is devoid of any visual styling and does not reflect well the experience of interacting with the full design.</li>

<li>It can be used interchangeably with card sorting.</li>
