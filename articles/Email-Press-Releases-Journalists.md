<span class="article-summary-label">

Summary:&nbsp;

</span>3 design guidelines for sending press releases to journalists by email.

</div>

<div class="article-body">

<p style="background: yellow;">This article is excerpted from the report on <a href="http://www.nngroup.com/reports/pr-websites/">PR on Websites</a>. (The full report is available as a <strong>free download</strong>.)<br/>

See also: <a href="http://www.nngroup.com/articles/press-area-usability/">summary of this research on how journalists use PR sites</a>.</p>

<p>The goal of this study was to review organizations’ public relations on the Web. During the study, journalists offered information about how they use press releases that are e-mailed to them, and which presentation is most effective.</p>

<p>Several journalists said that they didn’t use most of the press releases that were e-mailed to them. Many said they read a very small percentage of them, and still others said they read them all. Journalists in the last group we tested, however, received fewer than 10 releases per day.</p>

<p>Note that unsolicited releases via e-mail create a very different reaction from when journalists seek out releases on a specific topic.</p>

<h4>PR Guideline #101. In the e-mail, provide a short, clear subject line.</h4>

<p>Several journalists said they scan the subject line of the press releases in their inbox, and delete those that don’t quickly make a point.</p>

<ul>

<li><em>“Last night I got 85 e-mail messages, a lot of those were press releases. Only two were people with whom I have correspondences, the rest were press releases. ... I browse through them because I don’t want to miss anything. It may be a rare case. I only get a story from about 1%.”</em></li>

</ul>

<h4>PR Guideline #102. Send the e-mail from a person that the journalist is most likely to know or remember. Do not send it from a generic e-mail address.</h4>

<p>Journalists said they are more likely to open emails from people they know.</p>

<ul>

<li><em>“I get a lot. It is all about the subject line and the person who sent it to me. It really helps to know the person who sent it to me.”</em></li>

<li><em>“AOL has a clipping service, so I am getting 75 per day from that. I get a mix from the clipping service and PR people. I have been freelance for four years now. That makes me a little harder to find. As a freelancer, companies don’t know what to do with you as much.”</em></li>

</ul>

<h4>PR Guideline #103. Do not send more than one press release per e-mail.</h4>

<p>A few users said press releases are easier to deal with when they get one per e-mail.</p>

<ul>

<li><em>“There are clipping services – the most useless of them is the e-mail with 20 press releases in one file. I don’t use a lot of those ideas. I’d rather have 20 emails so I can glance through the subject and just delete them.”</em></li>

</ul>

<p>(See separate guidelines for presenting <a href="http://www.nngroup.com/articles/online-press-releases-and-news/">press releases on your own website</a>.)</p>
